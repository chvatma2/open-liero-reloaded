# 0.2's graphics on Windows at 1080p (2026-10-23)

The deferred Windows measurement of 0.2's graphics issues
(`docs/deferred-verification.md`'s `needs-bench` rows): every lit scene
they name, on the native build, at 1920 × 1080, with DX12 and Vulkan, on
the desktop GPU and on the integrated GPU (the laptop proxy).

**Method.** `main` at aea78eb3 with 242b5d32's fix (the Windows olr-cli
had not built since #34). Each scene is an 8-bot chaos match, 30 seconds,
seed 1 (`olr-cli stress --worms 8 --seconds 30 --seed 1 --record`), drawn
offscreen by `olr-cli gpu-bench --sizes 1920x1080 --probe-passes 0
--start-tick 100 --frames 2800` (ticks 100 to 2900, timestamp queries).
Without `--look` a scene draws its level's own look; `--look` replaces it
with the synthetic `bench_look`. Every point ran 3 times back to back on
an idle PC; the tables give the medians of the 3 runs' p50 and p99 in ms.
"Frame p99" is the tick and the frame together (`total_ms`). The machine:
Ryzen 5 7600X, RTX 5070 Ti, and the CPU's AMD Radeon graphics
(`--adapter integrated`). 138 runs on `main`, none failed.

## What it means

- **A desktop GPU has room to spare.** Every lit scene costs 1.6 to 2.5 ms
  of GPU at p50 and at most 3 ms at p99 on the RTX; with the tick, the
  frame's p99 stays under 4.8 ms (4 views on DX12, the worst), inside
  144 Hz's 6.9 ms on both backends.
- **An integrated GPU cannot draw a lit level today.** The same scenes
  take 61 to 86 ms with their own looks and 86 to 121 ms with `--look`:
  8 to 16 frames a second. Unlit, the same GPU draws OLX's levels in
  0.5 to 0.6 ms. Plan 15a measured 10 to 12 ms here with `--look`
  (`envelope.md`, a different replay) before plan 15c gave the glow light
  one cell a world pixel (`GI_CELL_BUDGET` 2^20: four times Castle
  Strike Reloaded's cells).
- **The radiance cascades are the frame.** They are 75 to 92 % of the GPU
  time on both GPUs (1.45 of 1.92 ms on the RTX, 55 of 68 ms on the
  integrated GPU), the jump flood a further 5 to 10 %. With one view every other
  pass group is under 0.05 ms on the RTX and under 1 ms on the integrated GPU.
  This settles `2026-10-graphics-candidates.md`'s "after the Windows table
  confirms the glow light's share": its candidate A (static and dynamic
  glow light) and D (per tick, or the upper cascades every other frame)
  are the levers; B (fewer flood passes, 4 of 12) is worth a few percent at most.
- **A fresh profile's default match is lit.** Liero v1.0 Reloaded on OLX's
  Liero Factory (#21) costs 1.75 ms on the RTX but 75 to 86 ms on the
  integrated GPU, against 0.5 ms for Classic on the same level.
- **The 0.2 features besides the glow light cost next to nothing.** Heat,
  even with the hostile mod heating every projectile at the caps (#42), is
  at most 0.07 ms p99 on the RTX; ambient occlusion (#10) adds 0.01 to
  0.04 ms; a distant layer (#11) 0.01 ms; Liero v1.0 Reloaded's glowing
  sprites and explosion lights (#15, #19) 0.01 to 0.04 ms over Classic on
  the same level; split screen (#26) 0.2 to 0.4 ms of GPU for 4 views (the
  glow light runs once), plus 0.2 to 0.5 ms of CPU at p50.
- **Unlit play is as fast as 0.1.0.** OLX's Castle Strike and Liero
  Factory with Classic draw in the same frame time as v0.1.0's build, a
  little less CPU (0.16 against 0.18 ms p50 on DX12).
- **#35's optimisations, on hardware:** 4 to 6 % of the GPU frame on both
  GPUs (Vulkan), the jump flood 11 % and the soak 33 % faster on the
  integrated GPU; lavapipe had measured 7.4 %. Main is a further 16 to
  20 % faster than #35's last commit on the RTX and 3 to 4 % on the
  integrated GPU (#53 took the soak's worm search out: 0.45 ms on the RTX
  is now 0.01 ms). Each build drew its own recording of the same match
  spec, so the matches are alike, not identical.
- **DX12 at #35's commits could not draw lit at all.** FXC refuses
  `olr-render soak`'s loop ("unable to unroll loop … 279 iterations") at
  a13c69c9 and 01503b4a, so the Windows game on DX12 failed on lit levels
  until #53 took that worm search out of the soak; main compiles it. #35's comparison is
  Vulkan only.

Not measured: #12's double-resolution skin (`gpu-bench` cannot pick a
skin; it needs the game's F2 frame time) and #6's blood at 500 % (the
envelope's `fx` sweep holds at 144 Hz up to 65 536 effects). Rows naming
"the commit before #N" (#10, #11, #19, #46, #53, #54, #56, #57) were
judged by their pass groups' size, not against builds of those commits:
each group they could slow is under 0.05 ms on the RTX.

## The envelope on the same day

`cargo xtask envelope` against `fixtures/bench/envelope/amd-ryzen-5-7600x-6-core-processor-12cpus.ron`
(measured 2026-10-16, not re-blessed): every sweep holds at 144 Hz and
60 Hz but art scale 8 (its memory and the content cap, as before). Ten of
50 measurements are beyond their noise: the simulation tick's p99 5 to
20 % slower (the pool sweep worst, 0.57 → 0.68 ms at 10 000 projectiles,
of a 10 ms budget) and the 28-tick rollback 18.6 % (3.08 ms, no budget);
the Windows and WSL frame p99s 3 to 29 % faster. The tick's growth is not
explained yet (0.2's simulation changes, the new keys and loadouts, were
not meant to cost time): a `cargo xtask bench` comparison against v0.1.0
says whether it is real. All of it is far inside the budgets.

## `cargo xtask full` on the same day

At 242b5d32, 13 of 16 steps passed: the tests, the bot grid, the GPU
tier (renderer GPU tests, render goldens, the 10-minute soak), audio,
`e2e`, `net-nat` (12 scenarios), determinism on Windows and arm64. Three
gates failed against baselines measured before most of 0.2
(`fixtures/bench/baselines.ron` and `stress.ron` 2026-10-13/16,
`fixtures/net/feel.ron` 2026-10-15); not re-blessed, not re-run:

- `bench`: `olr-cli/game/bots_8_tick` 14.75 µs against 8.81 (+67 %, the
  same in two later windows); every other bench within 10 %, the 1080p
  and 1440p frames 7 to 8 % faster.
- `stress`: MW 1.0 on Castle Strike, 32 chaos bots, pool 20 000: tick
  p50 762 µs against 690 (+10.5 %, its limit 10 %); the other three cases'
  ticks +2 to +6 %, their render 10 to 17 % faster.
- `net-feel`: the bad and awful profiles' bandwidth 20 % above the
  baseline (bad: down 40.0 KB/s against 32.1, up 9.4 against 8.3);
  rollbacks, corrections and stalls within their gates.

The bots' tick is the one to look into: the simulation's own ticks
(`olr-sim/tick/*`) are unchanged, so the time is in the bots' decisions
or the game loop around them (0.2's loadouts and keys touched both).
The bandwidth is plausibly the 16-bit keys (#23) and the Weapon 1-5
bits (#48) in every input; not checked.
